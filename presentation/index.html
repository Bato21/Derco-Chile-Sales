08 · Model B — predicting loss-making deals

Flag the money-losers before they close.

Supervised classifier on pre-sale features only — brand, channel, comuna, price, timing. Margin is excluded to avoid leakage (the target is derived from it). Dummy + logistic baselines → HistGradientBoosting, stratified hold-out + 5-fold CV.

10 · What it means & what to do

Five findings → one decision, three funded moves.

  • China won the shelf — 12% → 52% of sales. The core business, not a hedge.
  • Transactional, not relational — 86% buy once; the 14% Champions drive 26% of margin.
  • ~6.8 bn CLP leaks through 2.18% loss-making deals, concentrated in the own-store channel.
  • Single-brand dependence — Suzuki ≈ 39% of volume.
  • Geographic over-exposure — top-10 comunas ≈ 33% of sales.

1 · Retain

Fund a Champions-retention + one-timer-conversion loyalty CRM.

2 · Formalise China

Treat Chinese brands as a first-class portfolio — pricing, stock, marketing.

3 · Plug the leak

Classifier as a pre-approval check on the riskiest 10%, starting with own stores.

11 · Ethics, bias & limits

What this analysis cannot say.

  • Synthetic money — every CLP figure is a relative signal, not an audited financial. The direction is trustworthy; the level is not.
  • Privacy — the customer hash is re-identifiable, so we treat it as personal data; street addresses were excluded from every model.
  • Geographic bias — Santiago metro is over-represented, so “valuable customer” really means “urban customer”. A retention program must not redline rural comunas.
  • Moderate cluster quality — silhouette 0.39. The segments are a marketing convenience, not a law of nature.
  • Human in the loop — imbalanced target, no causal claims. The models advise; people decide. Re-check yearly.
